This document takes a somewhat sentimental tone to explain and, possibly and probably, answer certain questions that I am sure will be asked regarding this work.

I will begin this “so-called” essay by stating, right away, without hesitation and directly:

**a.** No, these results are not artificially or pompously fabricated merely to display a gigantic number. They correspond exactly to our parameters. For example, for seeds 1000 through 1099, we spent approximately 2 hours just installing the requirements on an Ubuntu partition (because, for some reason, it would not let me do this on Windows), and another 5 hours to perform ONLY the tests (time during which, ironically, I went outside to relax, because, objectively speaking, producing these tests requires RAM and CPU power; if I had been working, I would have subjected my laptop to torture :D). These results are 100% authentic and, fortunately, verifiable and reproducible by you! (I only hope you have a more powerful PC so that you do not spend nearly 7 hours just on the seeds.)

**b.** Yes, AI was used to complete this project. Aiming for as serious a grade as possible, anyone would be hypocritical to claim that they did not use AI (including us). We are now in an era of technology (and more recently, an era especially focused on what AI can do), and major technology companies, and not only them, require users to use this tool, directly or indirectly.

Just as on Google, if we search for something and have not disabled AI Overview (which 99% of us have not), we are shown what the AI says and are tempted to use it, logically, because the information is right under our noses: an innocuous temptation.

The difference is in how AI is used. If it is used for documentation, external sources to verify the eligibility of supported hypotheses, checking results (as we used it), and many other uses in this area, in my view this is not a problem; if it is used to copy the entire answer, to plagiarize, or to pretend that we understand what we did (when in reality we are on our phones and Claude or Gemini are solving everything for us), we have accomplished nothing of value and are merely pretending to be busy.

AI should be used intelligently as an aid, not as a replacement. I have always made the comparison that I am about to explain: when handheld calculators first appeared, they were initially used only by the social “elite,” then progressively became an exponentially sold product, for example in the United States.

Engineers and students began to use them very often (which created some controversy in schools, but this is not the hypothesis I want to emphasize), and an interesting situation arose: because they could perform algebraic calculations in milliseconds, people began to become dependent and unable to perform calculations mentally.

The same is true with AI, except that this technology has enviable capabilities: coding, mathematics in a billionth of a second, and much more. The AI bubble will soon “burst,” but until then, the fact that something is AI does not mean that it is immediately “fabricated”; we need to observe whether the subject is truly understood deeply, as the greatest modern mathematician, Tao, also said: “AI will become a useful tool for mathematicians.”

Amusingly, OpenAI released more than 700 pages of mathematical research, and now the implementation of “mathematics 2” is being requested, where the conclusion must be explained by humans, not artificially.
